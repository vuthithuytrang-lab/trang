1111: 1. What does office room furniture include?
1152: Office room furniture is the set of products arranged in a workspace to support daily tasks, document storage, internal communication, and receiving guests. Depending on the floor area, headcount, job roles, and operating model, each office room has different requirements for the quantity, style, and function of its furniture items.
1507: Office room furniture usually consists of 5 main groups:
1563: **Office desks: **The desk is the central area for computers, documents, and equipment used in daily work. Businesses should choose a desktop size, height, and style that suit the room area, working posture, and the amount of equipment to be arranged.
1812: **Office chairs: **The chair supports the body throughout working hours. Ergonomic chairs with adjustable height, seat depth, backrest, and armrests help users set up a posture that fits their body while reducing discomfort during long hours of sitting.
2096: **Document cabinets and storage shelves:** Cabinets and shelves help categorize and store files, contracts, internal documents, and personal belongings. Depending on usage needs, businesses can choose lockable closed cabinets, open shelves, mobile pedestals, or modular storage systems to optimize floor space.
2346: **Sofas and coffee tables: **This furniture group is usually placed in executive offices, manager offices, or private discussion areas. A sofa and coffee table create a space for receiving guests, short discussions, or work conversations in a more relaxed atmosphere than the main desk.
2611: **Support accessories: **Accessories such as partitions, cable management trays, desk lamps, power outlets, hooks, and side cabinets complete the room's functionality. Choosing suitable accessories also helps keep the desktop tidy and reduces inconveniences during work.
2869: A well-arranged office room should follow three principles: the right functions, a suitable size for the space, and consistent aesthetics. Businesses should avoid placing too much furniture that obstructs movement, while still ensuring enough storage space and essential amenities. A good balance between items helps the office maintain professionalism and meet long-term usage needs.

15209: 4. Common mistakes to avoid when buying office room furniture
15268: When investing in office room furniture, many businesses prioritize style and initial purchase cost without fully assessing usage needs, ergonomics, and the ability to change the space in the future. Unsuitable choices can increase adjustment or replacement costs. Here are seven mistakes to avoid:
15598: **Choosing furniture based on looks alone:** A beautifully designed product may not suit the nature of the work and actual usage needs. Businesses should evaluate dimensions, materials, adjustment functions, and compatibility with other items at the same time.
15852: **Ignoring ergonomics:** Chairs and desks directly affect the user's sitting posture. If a chair lacks adjustability or a desk has the wrong height, employees may quickly experience back, shoulder, and neck fatigue and find it hard to stay focused during long hours.
16114: **Choosing the wrong size for the room area:** Oversized furniture can narrow walkways and make it hard to pull out chairs or open cabinet drawers. Conversely, a desk that is too small limits the space for computers, documents, and equipment. Before buying, businesses should take measurements, determine operating clearances, and check the opening direction of each item.
16444: **Lack of storage space:** Offices without enough cabinets or shelves tend to have files, supplies, and shared equipment piled up on desks. This reduces work surface area and makes it difficult to find documents. Businesses should calculate storage capacity based on headcount, document types, and security needs.
16770: **Inconsistent furniture styles:** Choosing desks, chairs, sofas, and cabinets with different design languages can make the space look disjointed. Businesses should define a main color palette, material direction, and style before placing orders. Consistency does not require all products to be identical, but they should relate harmoniously in form and proportion.
17127: **Not planning for scalability:** Fixed desk systems that are hard to reconfigure can become a limitation when businesses add staff or reorganize departments. For shared offices, businesses should consider modular desk systems, flexible cabinets, and adaptable cable management solutions. Planning for expansion in advance helps reduce the cost of changing furniture in the future.
17506: **Overlooking quality and warranty policies:** A low purchase price does not fully reflect the total cost of ownership over the product's lifetime. Businesses should consider material durability, maintainability, spare parts supply, and warranty terms. Products with clear origins and appropriate after-sales service make management, repair, and replacement easier.

18020: 5.1. Should you choose a fixed desk or a height-adjustable desk?
18063: Fixed desks suit offices with a stable layout that need to optimize costs, while height-adjustable desks suit people who want to switch flexibly between sitting and standing while working.
18224: **Choose a fixed desk:** When the business needs to furnish in large quantities, has a limited budget, or is furnishing meeting rooms and work areas that rarely change.
18361: **Choose a height-adjustable desk: **When users work long hours at the computer, need to change posture frequently, or work in a flexible office where many employees share one desk.
